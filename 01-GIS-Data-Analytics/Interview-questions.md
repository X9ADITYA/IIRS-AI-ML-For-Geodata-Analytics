# Lesson 1 — GIS Data Analytics
# Interview Questions & Answers

## 1. What is Geospatial Technology?

Geospatial technology refers to technologies used to collect, manage, analyze, and visualize information associated with locations on Earth.

Major areas include:

- GIS / Spatial Analytics
- GNSS & Positioning
- Earth Observation
- Scanning technologies

---

## 2. What is GIS?

GIS stands for Geographic Information System.

It is a system used to work with geographically referenced information and perform operations such as data management, spatial analysis, and visualization.

---

## 3. What are the major components of Geospatial Technology introduced in the lesson?

The lesson introduces:

1. GIS / Spatial Analytics
2. GNSS & Positioning
3. Earth Observation
4. Scanning

---

## 4. What is GIS / Spatial Analytics?

GIS / Spatial Analytics involves working with geographic information and analyzing data according to its spatial characteristics.

GIS can be implemented through:

- Desktop systems
- Web/cloud platforms
- Mobile/tablet applications

---

## 5. What is GNSS?

GNSS stands for Global Navigation Satellite System.

It is associated with positioning and navigation applications.

Examples of applications include:

- Navigation
- Positioning
- Indoor positioning

---

## 6. What is Earth Observation?

Earth Observation involves obtaining information about the Earth using observation technologies.

Examples include:

- Satellite remote sensing
- UAV/drone observation
- Aerial surveys

---

## 7. What is LiDAR?

LiDAR stands for Light Detection and Ranging.

It is a scanning technology that uses laser-based measurements to obtain information about objects and surfaces.

---

## 8. What is TLS?

TLS stands for Terrestrial Laser Scanning.

It is a terrestrial scanning technology used to collect detailed information about objects and environments.

---

## 9. What is the difference between spatial data and attribute data?

### Spatial Data

Describes the location of a geographic feature.

It answers:

> Where?

### Attribute Data

Describes characteristics associated with that geographic location.

It answers questions such as:

> What? How much? When?

---

## 10. Give an example of spatial and attribute data.

Consider a school.

### Spatial Data

The geographic coordinates or location of the school.

### Attribute Data

Information such as:

- School name
- Number of students
- School type
- Area

---

## 11. What are GIS layers?

GIS layers are collections of geographic information grouped according to similar characteristics.

Examples include:

- Elevation
- Hydrography
- Water lines
- Sewer lines

---

## 12. What are the two major GIS data models?

The two major data models introduced are:

1. Vector data model
2. Raster data model

---

## 13. What is a vector data model?

The vector data model represents geographic features using geometric objects such as:

- Points
- Lines
- Polygons

---

## 14. What is a raster data model?

The raster data model represents geographic information using a grid of cells or pixels.

It is commonly used for imagery and continuous geographic information.

---

## 15. What is the difference between vector and raster data?

### Vector

Represents features using:

- Points
- Lines
- Polygons

### Raster

Represents information using:

- Grid cells
- Pixels

---

## 16. What is structured data?

Structured data is data organized according to a predefined structure, commonly using rows and columns.

Examples include:

- Database records
- Numerical data
- Spreadsheet data

---

## 17. What is semi-structured data?

Semi-structured data does not follow a strict tabular structure but contains some organizational information such as tags or metadata.

Examples include:

- Emails
- Hashtag-organized social media content
- Categorized files

---

## 18. What is unstructured data?

Unstructured data does not follow a predefined tabular or relational structure.

Examples include:

- Images
- Videos
- Audio
- Speech
- Documents

---

## 19. What is Big Data?

Big Data refers to datasets that are so large and complex that traditional database management systems and conventional data-processing applications may have difficulty processing them.

---

## 20. What are the major challenges of Big Data?

The lesson identifies challenges involving:

- Capture
- Curation
- Storage
- Search
- Sharing

- Visualization

---

## 21. What are the five characteristics of Big Data?

The five characteristics introduced are:

- Volume
- Velocity
- Variety
- Veracity
- Value

---

## 22. Explain Volume in Big Data.

Volume refers to the huge amount of data being generated, collected, and stored.

---

## 23. Explain Velocity in Big Data.

Velocity refers to the speed at which data is generated, accumulated, transferred, or processed.

---

## 24. Explain Variety in Big Data.

Variety refers to the different types and formats of data coming from different sources.

---

## 25. Explain Veracity in Big Data.

Veracity refers to inconsistencies, uncertainty, and reliability issues associated with data.

---

## 26. Explain Value in Big Data.

Value refers to the useful information or insights that can be extracted from data.

---

## 27. What is data collection?

Data collection is the process of obtaining data for analysis.

Data can be collected directly or obtained from existing sources.

---

## 28. What is primary data?

Primary data is data collected directly for a particular purpose.

Examples include:

- Surveys
- Questionnaires
- Field observations
- Measurements

---

## 29. What is secondary data?

Secondary data is data that already exists and is obtained from previously collected or published sources.

Examples include:

- Government census reports
- Research publications
- Company annual reports

---

## 30. What is data cleaning?

Data cleaning is the process of identifying and handling errors, inconsistencies, missing values, duplicates, and other data-quality problems before analysis.

---

## 31. Why is data cleaning important?

Data cleaning improves the quality and reliability of a dataset.

Poor-quality data can lead to incorrect analysis and unreliable conclusions.

---

## 32. What is Exploratory Data Analysis?

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is the process of examining a dataset to understand its structure, patterns, distributions, relationships, and unusual observations.

---

## 33. Why is visualization important in EDA?

Visualization helps analysts understand patterns, trends, relationships, and unusual observations more easily.

---

## 34. What is geoprocessing?

Geoprocessing refers to operations performed on geographic data to transform, analyze, manipulate, or derive new geographic information.

---

## 35. What is data visualization?

Data visualization is the graphical representation of data to make information, patterns, trends, and relationships easier to understand.

---

## 36. What is projection in GIS?

Projection refers to the method used to represent geographic coordinates from the Earth's curved surface on a flat map.

---

## 37. What is scale in GIS?

Scale represents the relationship between a distance on a map and the corresponding distance on the ground.

---

## 38. What is accuracy?

Accuracy describes how close a measured or represented value is to the true value.

---

## 39. What is resolution?

Resolution describes the level of detail represented by geographic data.

For raster data, spatial resolution is commonly associated with pixel size.

---

## 40. Why are projection, scale, accuracy, and resolution important?

These properties influence how geographic information is represented and interpreted.

Choosing appropriate properties is important for reliable GIS analysis.

---
